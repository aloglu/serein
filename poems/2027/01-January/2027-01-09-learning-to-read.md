---
title: Learning to Read
poet: Frances E. W. Harper
date: 2027-01-09
---

Very soon the Yankee teachers
::line |~1em| |<Came down and set up school;|
But, oh! how the Rebs did hate it,—
::line |~1em| |<It was agin’ their rule.|

Our masters always tried to hide
::line |~1em| |<Book learning from our eyes;|
Knowledge didn’t agree with slavery—
::line |~1em| |<’Twould make us all too wise.|

But some of us would try to steal
::line |~1em| |<A little from the book,|
And put the words together,
::line |~1em| |<And learn by hook or crook.|

I remember Uncle Caldwell,
::line |~1em| |<Who took pot-liquor fat|
And greased the pages of his book,
::line |~1em| |<And hid it in his hat.|

And had his master ever seen
::line |~1em| |<The leaves upon his head,|
He’d have thought them greasy papers,
::line |~1em| |<But nothing to be read.|

And there was Mr. Turner’s Ben,
::line |~1em| |<Who heard the children spell,|
And picked the words right up by heart,
::line |~1em| |<And learned to read ’em well.|

Well, the Northern folks kept sending
::line |~1em| |<The Yankee teachers down;|
And they stood right up and helped us,
::line |~1em| |<Though Rebs did sneer and frown.|

And, I longed to read my Bible,
::line |~1em| |<For precious words it said;|
But when I begun to learn it,
::line |~1em| |<Folks just shook their heads,|

And said there is no use trying,
::line |~1em| |<Oh! Chloe, you’re too late;|
But as I was rising sixty,
::line |~1em| |<I had no time to wait.|

So I got a pair of glasses,
::line |~1em| |<And straight to work I went,|
And never stopped till I could read
::line |~1em| |<The hymns and Testament.|

Then I got a little cabin
::line |~1em| |<A place to call my own—|
And I felt as independent
::line |~1em| |<As the queen upon her throne.|
