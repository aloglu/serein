---
title: Ode on a Grecian Urn
poet: John Keats
date: 2026-12-11
---

::line |~3em| |<1\.|

Thou still unravish’d bride of quietness,
::line |~1em| |<Thou foster-child of silence and slow time,|
Sylvan historian, who canst thus express
::line |~1em| |<A flowery tale more sweetly than our rhyme:|
What leaf-fring’d legend haunts about thy shape
::line |~1em| |<Of deities or mortals, or of both,|
::line |~2em| |<In Tempe or the dales of Arcady?|
::line |~1em| |<What men or gods are these? What maidens loth?|
What mad pursuit? What struggle to escape?
::line |~2em| |<What pipes and timbrels? What wild ecstasy?|

::line |~3em| |<2\.|

Heard melodies are sweet, but those unheard
::line |~1em| |<Are sweeter; therefore, ye soft pipes, play on;|
Not to the sensual ear, but, more endear’d,
::line |~1em| |<Pipe to the spirit ditties of no tone:|
Fair youth, beneath the trees, thou canst not leave
::line |~1em| |<Thy song, nor ever can those trees be bare;|
::line |~2em| |<Bold Lover, never, never canst thou kiss,|
::line |~1em| |<Though winning near the goal—yet, do not grieve;|
She cannot fade, though thou hast not thy bliss,
::line |~2em| |<For ever wilt thou love, and she be fair!|

::line |~3em| |<3\.|

Ah, happy, happy boughs! that cannot shed
::line |~1em| |<Your leaves, nor ever bid the Spring adieu;|
And, happy melodist, unwearied,
::line |~1em| |<For ever piping songs for ever new;|
More happy love! more happy, happy love!
::line |~1em| |<For ever warm and still to be enjoy’d,|
::line |~2em| |<For ever panting, and for ever young;|
::line |~1em| |<All breathing human passion far above,|
That leaves a heart high-sorrowful and cloy’d,
::line |~2em| |<A burning forehead, and a parching tongue.|

::line |~3em| |<4\.|

Who are these coming to the sacrifice?
::line |~1em| |<To what green altar, O mysterious priest,|
Lead’st thou that heifer lowing at the skies,
::line |~1em| |<And all her silken flanks with garlands drest?|
What little town by river or sea shore,
::line |~1em| |<Or mountain-built with peaceful citadel,|
::line |~2em| |<Is emptied of this folk, this pious morn?|
::line |~1em| |<And, little town, thy streets for evermore|
Will silent be; and not a soul to tell
::line |~2em| |<Why thou art desolate, can e’er return.|

::line |~3em| |<5\.|

O Attic shape! Fair attitude! with brede
::line |~1em| |<Of marble men and maidens overwrought,|
With forest branches and the trodden weed;
::line |~1em| |<Thou, silent form, dost tease us out of thought|
As doth eternity: Cold Pastoral!
::line |~1em| |<When old age shall this generation waste,|
::line |~2em| |<Thou shalt remain, in midst of other woe|
::line |~1em| |<Than ours, a friend to man, to whom thou say’st,|
“Beauty is truth, truth beauty,”—that is all
::line |~2em| |<Ye know on earth, and all ye need to know.|
